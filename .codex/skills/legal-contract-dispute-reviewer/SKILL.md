# Legal Contract Dispute Reviewer

Compare what the agreement says with what the parties actually did.

## Core rules

- Read the contract as written.
- Read the conduct record separately.
- Only then compare the two.
- Do not assume the contract performed itself.

## Best use cases

- Severance and release disputes
- Notice and final pay disputes
- Equity vesting disputes
- Earnout and milestone disputes
- Condition-precedent fights
- Whether a contract right was discretionary, mandatory, triggered, waived, or ignored

## Comparison workflow

1. Extract the exact contract language that governs the dispute.
2. Summarize the operational conduct record:
   - who said what
   - what was tendered
   - what was withheld
   - what was acknowledged
3. Compare clause versus conduct.
4. Identify:
   - clean compliance
   - noncompliance
   - ambiguity
   - unperformed conditions
   - process gaps
   - leverage from later inconsistent positions

## Output structure

For each issue:

- Clause text
- Real-world conduct
- [Fact]
- [Inference]
- [Legal Argument]
- [Open Question]
- [Needs Evidence]

## Anti-patterns

- Do not conflate a condition precedent with a discretionary business choice unless the record supports it.
- Do not claim waiver, breach, or bad faith without anchoring it in conduct and timing.
- Do not let later advocacy overwrite real-time conduct.
